Topic: Stroke Risk Assessment
Target Output: Low, Medium, High Risk

1. CORE VARIABLE ASSUMPTIONS (HEURISTIC BASELINE)
This fuzzy inference system relies on generalized medical consensus regarding cardiovascular and stroke risk factors. The baseline rules operate on the following isolated assumptions:
* Age: Stroke risk compounding correlates heavily with age. 'Senior' is a baseline multiplier for risk, while 'Young' acts as a dampener.
* Hypertension (HT): Chronic high blood pressure is a leading cause of strokes. 'HT_Present' is treated as a primary risk accelerator.
* Glucose Level: High glucose indicates prediabetes or diabetes, which damages blood vessels over time. 'Diabetic' is a severe risk factor.
* BMI: Obesity places mechanical and metabolic stress on the heart. 'Obese' compounds other risk factors, while 'Low/Normal' mitigates them.
* Smoking: Tobacco damages blood vessels and raises blood pressure. 'High' (active smoker) acts as a catalyst when combined with Age or HT.

2. CATEGORICAL LOGIC & RULE BREAKDOWN

--- HIGH RISK ASSUMPTIONS ---
Logic: High risk is assigned when a primary risk accelerator (Hypertension or Diabetes) is combined with an age-based vulnerability or a compounding lifestyle factor (Obesity, Heavy Smoking).

* Rule 1 (Senior + HT Present): Advanced age combined with high blood pressure is the most standard indicator of imminent stroke risk.
* Rule 2 (Diabetic + Obese): Represents severe metabolic syndrome. The compounding vascular damage from diabetes combined with obesity warrants a high warning.
* Rule 3 (Senior + Smoke High + HT Present): A "triple threat" scenario. Advanced age, active arterial damage from smoking, and high blood pressure guarantee high risk.
* Rule 4 (Diabetic + HT Present): Combines two of the most destructive factors for blood vessel integrity (glucose damage + pressure damage).
* Rule 5 (Middle Age + Smoke High + HT Present): Even without being a 'Senior', the combination of active heavy smoking and hypertension creates premature high risk.

--- MEDIUM RISK ASSUMPTIONS ---
Logic: Medium risk is assigned when there is a mix of positive and negative factors, or when an individual possesses moderate, non-critical risk markers that require monitoring but are not immediately life-threatening alone.

* Rule 6 (Middle Age + HT Absent + Smoke Mod): The patient is aging and has a moderate smoking history, but normal blood pressure keeps the risk from escalating to high.
* Rule 7 (Senior + Glucose Normal + HT Absent): The patient is elderly (inherent risk), but excellent metabolic and cardiovascular markers downgrade the threat to medium.
* Rule 8 (Overweight + Smoke Mod): Two lifestyle warning signs are present, indicating a trajectory toward high risk if not addressed.
* Rule 9 (Middle Age + Prediabetic): A classic warning stage. The body is beginning to show metabolic stress, but severe damage has not yet occurred.
* Rule 10 (Young + HT Present): Youth protects the vascular system, but premature hypertension is highly abnormal and flags a medium risk for early intervention.

--- LOW RISK ASSUMPTIONS ---
Logic: Low risk is assigned when age is not a primary factor and metabolic/cardiovascular health indicators are firmly in the normal/absent ranges.

* Rule 11 (Young + HT Absent): The optimal healthy baseline. Youth combined with normal blood pressure indicates highly resilient blood vessels.
* Rule 12 (Glucose Normal + BMI Low): Represents excellent metabolic health, drastically lowering the baseline probability of a stroke.
* Rule 13 (Young + Smoke Low): A young individual with no history of smoking lacks the primary triggers for arterial damage.
* Rule 14 (Middle Age + Glucose Normal + HT Absent): Even as the patient ages, the absence of diabetes and hypertension keeps their risk profile low.
* Rule 15 (Smoke Low + HT Absent + BMI Low): The "perfect lifestyle" rule. Regardless of other factors, avoiding smoking, maintaining low weight, and having normal blood pressure ensures a minimal risk profile.
